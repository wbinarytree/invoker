# Abilities

Abilities are unique battlefield skills available to heroes and creeps, ranging from simple passive effects through destructive energy explosions to complex terrain-changing feats. Every hero has at least four abilities: at least three basic abilities and an ultimate. Heroes can assign ability points when they level up; each ability level strengthens the ability and may also increase its mana cost.

Abilities may consume mana or enter cooldown when used. An ability cannot be used again until its cooldown expires. Low-cooldown abilities are sometimes called **spammable** because they can be used frequently. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/abilities@2354873]

## Upgrading abilities

Heroes earn one ability point whenever they level up until their abilities are fully upgraded. A point can unlock an ability or upgrade one already learned. At the start of the game, every ability is locked, while the hero starts with one ability point that can be spent at any time. Locked abilities cannot be used and have no effect.

Once unlocked, a basic ability can normally be upgraded 3 more times for a total of 4 levels. An ultimate can normally be upgraded 2 more times for a total of 3 levels at hero levels 6/12/18.

| Ability level | Hero level for regular ability | Hero level for ultimate* |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 6 |
| 2 | 3 | 12 |
| 3 | 5 | 18 |
| 4 | 7 | - |
| 5 | 9 | - |
| 6 | 11 | - |
| 7 | 13 | - |

Exceptions:

- Invoker can upgrade his basic abilities 6 times, for a total of 7 levels.
- Meepo can upgrade his ultimate at levels 4/11/18, or 3/10/17/24 with More Meepo.

Upgrading may increase damage, extend an ongoing effect such as a disable, or otherwise improve the ability. It may also increase or decrease mana cost. Each level of Earthshaker’s Fissure increases its damage and stun duration but also increases its mana cost.

Abilities can also be strengthened through:

- **Spell Amplification**, which slightly increases their damage.
- **Aghanim’s Scepter** and **Aghanim’s Shard**, which uniquely enhance each hero by improving an ultimate, improving one or more basic abilities, or granting an entirely new ability.
- Abilities that improve a hero’s other abilities; Ogre Magi’s Multicast, for example, makes his other 3 abilities more powerful. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/abilities@2354873#Upgrading_Abilities]

## Basic abilities

A basic ability can gain a new level once every 2 hero levels. A level 2 hero therefore cannot upgrade a learned basic ability to level 2. Every 2 levels, the hero’s maximum basic-ability level increases by 1. Ultimates can be upgraded only every 6 levels starting at level 6 and are usually more powerful than basic abilities. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/abilities@2354873#Basic_Abilities]

## Ultimates

A hero’s last ability is called an **ultimate** and can usually be trained at levels 6, 12, and 18. The sole exception is Meepo’s Divided We Stand, which can be upgraded at levels 4, 11, and 18, or at 3, 10, 17, and 24 with More Meepo.

Ultimates may be high-impact abilities that define a hero’s strengths and playstyle, often combining devastating effects with long cooldowns. Others primarily synergize with the hero’s remaining abilities. Some have weaker effects but relatively short, spammable cooldowns. Ultimates can also be passive. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/abilities@2354873#Ultimates]

## Sub-abilities

Some abilities come with sub-abilities. These extra abilities usually do nothing independently and generally become active after their associated main ability is cast; some instead set up other abilities. They can activate another ability’s effects on demand, manipulate an ability, or cancel it completely. They are usually innate or unlocked by learning a specific ability.

Because sub-abilities usually have very low or no cooldown, they generally do not trigger effects that react to ability use, including charge gain from Magic Wand-based items and Arcane Curse’s per-cast penalty. Spell Steal and Morph automatically acquire a sub-ability together with its main ability. If its caster lacks the item required to unlock it, such as Aghanim’s Scepter or Aghanim’s Shard, the sub-ability does nothing. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/abilities@2354873#Sub-abilities]

## Linked abilities

Linked abilities are learned automatically when their counterpart is learned, and vice versa. A linked ability usually has its own ability slot and automatically levels with its counterpart. Linked abilities differ from toggleable abilities with different states, such as Berserker’s Rage, and from sub-abilities. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/abilities@2354873#Linked_Abilities]

## Transformation abilities

Transformation abilities generally change a hero’s form while granting other stat bonuses. Created illusions can benefit from the hero’s current state for the transformation buff’s remaining duration. These abilities have a transformation time and may modify melee or ranged attack-range properties or other stats.

For Spell Steal and Ability Draft, the bonuses and reductions of multiple transformation abilities fully stack with one another:

- All attack-damage bonuses.
- All movement-speed effects, including Shapeshift haste.
- All attack modifiers.
- All effects altering health, mana, or their regeneration.
- Attack-range definitions and notes.
- Items with different values for melee and ranged heroes, such as Manta Style, treat the caster’s ranged type according to the ability notes.

Hex sources generally are not transformation abilities because they only change the unit’s current model. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/abilities@2354873#Transformation_Abilities]

# Ability types

An ability’s type describes how its effects are applied. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/abilities@2354873#Ability_Types]

## Active

Active abilities must be used to apply their effects. They may consume mana, have cooldowns, and usually use a targeting method. Most abilities are active and can be activated with their associated hotkey. If an ability can target its caster, double-tapping its hotkey immediately activates it on the caster.

An active ability’s icon has a bevel that makes it resemble a button. When the ability is used and enters its cast animation, the bevel turns green and the icon shifts to resemble a pressed button. While a targeted ability is selected, its icon and bevel appear brighter until it is deselected or casting begins. Its hotkey appears in the icon’s top-left corner.

| Example active ability |
|---|
| Weaver – Shukuchi |
| Ursa – Overpower |
| Necrophos – Reaper’s Scythe |

[corpus:liquipedia_dota2/abilities@2354873#Active]

## Passive

Passive abilities either apply their effects permanently as soon as they are learned or activate automatically when learned and their requirements are met. Requirements may include attacking a unit, casting a spell, or being attacked. Most passives consume no mana and have no cooldown; Wraith King’s Reincarnation is an exception. Most, but not all, heroes have at least one passive.

Passive icons have no bevel and appear pressed into the HUD. Because passives cannot be used manually, their appearance never changes. Their icon is displayed only after the ability is learned.

| Example passive ability |
|---|
| Wraith King – Reincarnation |
| Crystal Maiden – Arcane Aura |
| Drow Ranger – Marksmanship |

[corpus:liquipedia_dota2/abilities@2354873#Passive]

## Autocast

Autocast abilities are special active abilities that can be toggled on or off or cast manually. Right-clicking the ability toggles autocast; **Alt+Hotkey** is the default toggle hotkey. Silences and stuns do not prevent toggling autocast, although silence prevents the ability itself from being used like any other spell.

Most autocast spells are active attack modifiers. When toggled on, they modify attacks whenever possible; Drow Ranger’s Frost Arrows, for example, is used whenever she attacks a target. When toggled off, they are not used and consume no mana. Manually casting an active attack modifier causes it to be treated partly as a spell rather than an attack, preventing nearby enemy creeps from aggroing. This is known as **orb walking**.

Some autocast abilities are regular abilities rather than attack modifiers. When toggled on, they cast automatically when their requirements are met. Ogre Magi, for example, automatically casts Bloodlust on nearby allied heroes.

Autocast icons resemble ordinary active-ability icons. Their bevel turns white when selected and green when casting, except that attack modifiers do not turn green. A thicker second bevel sits behind the first and glows orange while autocast is enabled. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/abilities@2354873#Autocast]

# Targeting types

Active and autocast abilities use different targeting methods according to their effects. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/abilities@2354873#Targeting_Types]

## No target

No-target abilities cast immediately when their button is pressed. Many have no cast time, but they do not have an instant cast time. They cannot directly target a unit or point and usually affect the caster, units around the caster, an area around the caster, or the entire map.

| Example |
|---|
| Mirana – Leap |
| Slardar – Slithereen Crush |
| Zeus – Thundergod’s Wrath |

[corpus:liquipedia_dota2/abilities@2354873#No_Target]

### Toggle

Toggle abilities are always non-targetable. They apply periodic effects and often drain mana or health while enabled. Their effects include damaging or healing nearby allies, switching attack types, and manipulating the unit’s other abilities. A mana-draining toggle turns itself off when insufficient mana remains.

Only the player can forcibly disable a toggle, by pressing its button again. Like regular spells, toggles cannot be turned on or off while the unit is affected by a disable that prevents ability use. Once enabled, however, they persist through every form of disable, including silence. All toggleable abilities have instant cast time by default. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/abilities@2354873#Toggle]

## Target point

Point-target abilities require the caster to choose a point or area. Pressing the hotkey changes the cursor into a crosshair that determines the cast location or direction. Some abilities use the point only to determine the direction in which an effect is launched; others apply an effect at the exact selected point.

| Example |
|---|
| Techies – Remote Mines |
| Spectre – Spectral Dagger |
| Morphling – Waveform |

[corpus:liquipedia_dota2/abilities@2354873#Target_Point]

### Target area

Area-target abilities operate like point-target abilities but require a whole area rather than a single point. Pressing the spell button changes the cursor into an indicator showing the affected area. These abilities usually affect the whole area or every unit within it. Some apply their effects once when cast; others create a lasting effect that also affects units entering afterward.

| Example |
|---|
| Crystal Maiden – Crystal Nova |
| Alchemist – Acid Spray |
| Faceless Void – Chronosphere |

[corpus:liquipedia_dota2/abilities@2354873#Target_Area]

### Vector targeting

A vector-targeted spell requires two locations. The first determines its starting location and the second its direction.

| Casting method | Procedure |
|---|---|
| Regular cast | Select the ability with its hotkey, move the cursor to the desired starting location, and hold the left mouse button. Move the cursor toward the desired direction, then release the left mouse button. |
| Quick cast on key up | Move the cursor to the desired starting location, then press and hold the ability hotkey to set that location. Move the cursor toward the desired direction, then release the hotkey. |
| Quick cast on key down | Move the cursor to the desired starting location, press the ability hotkey to set it, and move the cursor toward the desired direction. Press the left mouse button to select the direction. |

## Target unit

Unit-target abilities require the caster to target a unit directly and cannot target the ground. Pressing the hotkey changes the cursor into a crosshair used to select the unit. Most affect only that target; abilities applying their effect to one unit are often called **single-target abilities**.

If the target is outside cast range, the caster follows it until it enters range or can no longer be targeted, such as when it becomes invisible.

Some point-target abilities can also target a unit directly. The spell is cast toward the targeted unit’s current location. If the unit is outside cast range, the caster follows until it is in range and then casts at its position.

If the target moves during the cast animation, the spell is still cast on it or launched toward it if the effect travels. The spell’s actual effects do not home in on the target.

| Example |
|---|

Their targeting cursor resembles that of a ground-targeted area spell. It has the same blue ring, with pulsing gapped rings moving outward from the center. A small yellow reticule in the center indicates that a unit must be targeted.
