# Creep Stacking

## Overview

Creep stacking draws neutral creeps beyond their camp boundaries so a new set can spawn alongside any remaining creeps, increasing the number of units in the area. Neutral creeps generally spawn in the first minute and every minute thereafter: 1:00, 2:00, 3:00 etc. A new set cannot spawn while a unit such as a player or ward is within the camp boundary. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/creep_stacking@2307142]

Stacking is time-efficient because it increases the gold available to a team. A support who creates a substantial stack can provide gold for teammates while receiving a stacking bonus of 30% of the gold and experience bounty. If the enemy team reaches the camps first, stacking can work against the team and the stacker receives no bonus bounty. The stacker also receives 20% reduced gold and experience from those creeps, so it is more rewarding for one player—most often a support—to stack and another to clear them. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/creep_stacking@2307142]

## Basics

To stack a camp, attack one of its units or walk close to it. The neutral creeps attempt to chase for a few seconds before returning to their initial positions. Most camps can be stacked around the 54-55 second mark, although the timing varies with the spawn-box size and the units involved. When performed correctly, a new set spawns at the minute mark. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/creep_stacking@2307142#Basics]

The process can be repeated, but later timings may differ because some creeps have movement-speed auras and the creeps can obstruct one another. Every neutral camp can be stacked indefinitely as long as all creeps escape its boundaries. An extremely stacked Dire Ancient Camp yields about 1500 gold. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/creep_stacking@2307142#Basics]

## Camp Boundary Boxes

Practice stacking with the intended hero before an actual match; the custom game **Creep Stacking Training** can be used for practice. Required timings vary according to the camp type and how long its creeps chase, the neutrals currently present, their movement speeds, whether they remain until the hero leaves their attack range, and the hero’s attributes—including attack range when attacking to attract the creeps. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/creep_stacking@2307142#Camp_Boundary_Boxes]

Each camp has a **Base Stack Time**, modified as follows:

| Situation | Modification |
|---|---|
| Melee hero with a long-ranged neutral currently in the camp, such as Vhoul Assassin Camp or Troll Camp | subtract ≈:01 [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/creep_stacking@2307142#Camp_Boundary_Boxes] |
| Short-ranged ranged hero, such as Luna or Huskar, with a long-ranged creep currently in the camp | subtract ≈:00.25 [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/creep_stacking@2307142#Camp_Boundary_Boxes] |

## Advanced Techniques

Invisible camp boundaries extend upward as well as across the map. Certain heroes can stack very quickly; Clockwerk’s Rocket Flare can block a camp or stack it from anywhere if it damages the creeps. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/creep_stacking@2307142#Advanced_Techniques]

Ranged abilities allow some heroes to attack and stack creeps at little to no risk. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/creep_stacking@2307142#Safespotting]

### Creep Pulling

Lane creeps can absorb most of a neutral camp’s damage, although the technique is difficult. Attack the neutral camp at the -:17 mark and walk toward the incoming lane creeps, drawing the wave into the jungle. Creeps can then be pulled again toward a hard camp. If performed incorrectly, remaining creeps return to the lane and eventually push out the creep equilibrium. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/creep_stacking@2307142#Creep_Pulling]

## Tips

The Ghost reduces movement speed by 20%, making the easy camp harder to stack when present. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/creep_stacking@2307142#Tips]

The medium camp closest to the top T2 Dire tower can be pulled around the -:56 mark so the creeps are moving down the steps and lack vision of their camp. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/creep_stacking@2307142#Tips]
