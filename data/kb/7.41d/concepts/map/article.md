# Map

The Map is the playing field for every Dota 2 match. It comprises a Radiant side and a Dire side; victory requires destroying the opposing Ancient, an important building at the center of that team’s base. The interface represents the map through the minimap, and the version 7.41 map has an overhead view. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137]

## Factions

Each faction occupies a diagonal half of the map, with the river separating them through the middle. The Radiant base is in the lower-left corner and the Dire base in the upper-right corner. Each Ancient lies within a stronghold at the heart of its faction’s territory. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Factions]

## Lanes

The map has three lanes—top, bottom, and middle—each leading to the opposing base and guarded by towers. Most early-game laning-phase gameplay centers on these lanes. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Lanes]

Radiant’s bottom lane and Dire’s top lane are their safe lanes. Creeps meet close to the towers, which offer greater enemy protection; the resulting added protection against ganking makes these lanes recommended for beginners. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Lanes]

## River

The river separates the factions’ sides and is surrounded by high ground that blocks vision on both banks. It interacts with two spells: Water Regen heals 10 HP per second and restores 5 mana per second while in the river; Guardian Sprint amplifies its movement-speed bonus there and, when upgraded, also provides status resistance. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#River]

With Aghanim’s Scepter, Slardar can manually create river spots: casting Slithereen Crush creates a puddle around Slardar that counts as river. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#River]

Bounty Runes spawn every 3 minutes and grant a bounty; Power-up Runes spawn every 2 minutes and grant bonuses. From the game’s start, Bounty Runes spawn at four locations: two in the river, one on the Radiant side, and one on the Dire side. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Runes]

### Roshan

Roshan is an exceptionally strong creep at the river’s western end and usually requires more than one player to fight. Killing him grants the entire team gold and causes him to drop 5 different items according to death count. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Roshan]

## Jungle

The Jungle comprises the forested areas between lanes. Most neutral creeps are found there and can be killed for gold and experience. Leveling through jungle creeps instead of lane creeps is called Jungling. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Jungle]

## Buildings

Buildings are units not controlled by players. At match start, fixed sets spawn at fixed locations in both bases. The factions have identical building sets whose appearances differ; these buildings primarily serve defensive roles and constitute the game’s main objective. Neutral buildings elsewhere on the map can be conquered or otherwise used by both teams. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Buildings]

### Faction Buildings

All faction buildings share the same base properties, are immune to most abilities, and can be affected by only a few. Some destroyed buildings award a team Gold bounty, while others award a last-hit bounty. A building below 10% of maximum health can be denied to reduce or prevent enemy gold bounties, but Ancients cannot be denied. Buildings grant no experience, are permanently lost when destroyed, and do not respawn. Each faction has 26 buildings. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Faction_Buildings]

#### Towers

Towers attack any non-neutral enemy unit entering their range. Each faction has three towers in each of the three lanes plus two guarding its Ancient, for 11 towers total. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Towers]

| Tier | Location |
|---|---|
| Towers (Tier 1) | At the end of each lane. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Towers] |
| Towers (Tier 2) | Halfway through each lane. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Towers] |
| Towers (Tier 3) | On top of the 3 ramps at each base. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Towers] |
| Towers (Tier 4) | In pairs in front of each Ancient. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Towers] |

#### Barracks

Barracks, commonly shortened to Rax or Racks, stand behind and are defended by the Tier 3 Tower in each respective lane. They need not be destroyed to make Tier 4 Towers vulnerable. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Barracks]

Each faction has two Barracks per lane and 6 total: 3 Melee Barracks for Melee and Flagbearer Creeps, and 3 Ranged Barracks for Ranged and Siege Creeps. In every lane for both factions, the Ranged Barracks is always left of the Melee Barracks. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Barracks]

#### Ancients

Ancients are massive structures inside the faction bases and the game’s main objective. They are also called Thrones; legacy DotA names call the Radiant Ancient the Tree and the Dire Ancient the Throne. Victory requires destroying the enemy Ancient while keeping the allied Ancient alive. Two Tier 4 Towers guard each Ancient, and both must be destroyed to remove its invulnerability. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Ancients]

#### Fountains

Fountains occupy the respawn areas of both bases. Rejuvenation Aura provides maximum health and mana regeneration to affected allied units. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Fountains]

#### Effigies

Effigy Buildings, or Filler Buildings, buffer the Tier 4 Towers by delaying lane creeps. As of 7.41d, each base has 7 Effigy Buildings distributed around its Ancient. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Effigies]

Five form a semicircle around the Ancient and can be customized with Effigy Blocks, changing their appearance and making them display a text notification when destroyed. This is purely cosmetic, and each player may customize one of those five buildings in their base. Radiant has two effigy-building models; Dire uses one model for all of them. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Effigies]

#### Defender’s Gates

Defender’s Gates are force fields in each faction’s main-base walls. Allies can path through them, while enemies are blocked completely. Each faction has 2, at the furthest edges of the base walls behind the top and bottom Tier 3 Towers. They connect to paths leading directly toward the Tormentors and are separated from the main lane by cliffs. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Defender's_Gates]

### Neutral Buildings

Neutral Buildings do not directly belong to either faction but can be captured or used by either. They are permanently invulnerable, cannot be destroyed, and are immune to every ability. They are always visible to both teams through the Fog of War. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Neutral_Buildings]

| Type | Rules |
|---|---|
| Capturable buildings | Players can conquer them for their faction, and the opposing faction can recapture them. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Neutral_Buildings] |
| True neutral buildings | Never belong to either faction and can be used by both. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Neutral_Buildings] |

#### Outposts

There are 4 Outposts: one in each team’s primary jungle near a Bounty Rune spot, and one beyond each safe lane near the map edge and a Tier 1 Tower. All initially belong to the team on their side. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Outposts]

An enemy can capture a primary-jungle Outpost after destroying any Tier 2 Tower. A team can reclaim its enemy-acquired Outpost without destroying an enemy Tier 2 Tower. Map-edge Outposts cannot be captured. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Outposts]

#### Twin Gates

The 2 Twin Gates occupy the north-west and south-east map corners near the Roshan pits. Despite using Radiant and Dire visual themes, they belong to neither faction and can be used equally by all players. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Twin_Gates]

#### Lotus Pools

The 2 Lotus Pools lie at the west and east map edges, closer to the north and south corners respectively. Despite using Radiant and Dire visual themes, they belong to neither faction and can be used equally by all players. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Lotus_Pools]

## Minimap

The Minimap occupies a corner of the head-up display. It provides an overhead view of the entire map and can issue certain commands. Constant minimap awareness is crucial to skilled play. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Minimap]

## Vision

Vision determines which map areas are visible to a player’s team; unseen areas are covered by the Fog of War. Allied heroes, units, and wards can grant vision, while trees, walls, and higher terrain obstruct it. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Vision]

Six gameplay-relevant height levels exist. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Terrain]

## Pathing

Whenever a player gives a move command, pathing finds the fastest possible route. Cliffs, Trees, Iron Branches, other units through Body Block, and Traps can block that route. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Pathing]

Cliffs are the primary map feature limiting movement and can be crossed only with Forced Movement, Blinks, or Unobstructed Movement. Trees largely define lane and jungle contours, can be destroyed through various means, and permit juking in the Fog of War. Body blocking helps maintain favorable lane creep equilibrium or prevent an enemy from escaping until allies arrive. Traps primarily cut off chasing or escaping heroes’ routes but can also enable unconventional pulling. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Pathing]

## Size

The map measures approximately 11,000 × 11,000 game units. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#Trivia]

## History

The Dota 2 map ultimately derives from the StarCraft custom map *Aeon of Strife*, which featured multiple lanes and a final destruction objective. Its current diagonal format originated in *Defense of the Ancients*, the Warcraft 3 custom map on which Dota 2 is based. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/map@2402137#History]
