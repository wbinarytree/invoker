# Lanes

A lane is one of the three main paths connecting the two factions; each has its own advantages and disadvantages. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818]

## Terminology

Lanes are named for their geographic locations. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Terminology]

| Lane | Location |
|---|---|
| Top Lane | Runs along the left and top edges of the map. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Terminology] |
| Middle Lane | Runs diagonally through the center of the map. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Terminology] |
| Bottom Lane | Runs along the bottom and right edges of the map. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Terminology] |

The Top and Bottom lanes are collectively called the **Side Lanes**. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Terminology]

### Top Lane

The **Top Lane**, often shortened to **Top**, connects Radiant’s north ramp to Dire’s west ramp along the west (left) and north (top) sides of the map. From Radiant’s north entrance it proceeds straight north, turns 90° east at the height of Dire’s west ramp, and continues to that ramp. It is Radiant’s Safe Lane and Dire’s Off Lane. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Top_Lane]

### Middle Lane

The **Middle Lane**, often shortened to **Mid Lane** or **Mid**, connects Radiant’s north-east ramp to Dire’s south-west ramp, running diagonally through the center of the map. Unlike the other lanes, it remains straight throughout and is the shortest of the three. From Radiant’s north-east entrance it proceeds straight north-east to Dire’s ramp. The lane has the same role for both teams. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Middle_Lane]

### Bottom Lane

The **Bottom Lane**, often shortened to **Bot Lane** or **Bot**, connects Radiant’s east ramp to Dire’s south ramp along the east (right) and south (bottom) sides of the map. From Radiant’s east entrance it proceeds straight east, turns 90° north at the height of Dire’s south ramp, and continues to that ramp. It is Radiant’s Off Lane and Dire’s Safe Lane. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Bottom_Lane]

### Safe Lane

Safe Lane refers to the part of the Bottom Lane within Radiant’s side of the map and the part of the Top Lane within Dire’s side. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Safe_Lane]

| Faction | Safe Lane path |
|---|---|
| Radiant | Starts at the east ramp, reaches toward the corner, turns 90°, and continues north to the south-east river ramp. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Safe_Lane] |
| Dire | Starts at the west ramp, reaches toward the corner, turns 90°, and continues south to the north-west river ramp. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Safe_Lane] |

| Safe Lane trait |
|---|
| It is adjacent to the primary jungle, giving the team easy access to an alternative farming location during the laning phase. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Safe_Lane] |
| If the team has a jungler, that player can aid the lane during emergencies. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Safe_Lane] |
| Its Tier 1 Tower is immediately before the lane’s 90° corner, while the opposing team’s first tower is behind the river ramp. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Safe_Lane] |
| Without interference, lane creeps meet within the respective faction’s side of the map, closer to that faction’s tower. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Safe_Lane] |
| The river ramps are farther from the Safe Lane Tier 1 Towers, making river ganks more difficult. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Safe_Lane] |
| The Safe Lane covers more than half of the Top or Bottom Lane—roughly 60%—because it extends beyond the 90° turn. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Safe_Lane] |
| It is generally considered more secure for players than the other Side Lane, hence the name “Safe” Lane. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Safe_Lane] |

### Off Lane

The **Off Lane**, also called the **Hard Lane**, refers to the part of the Top Lane within Radiant’s side of the map and the part of the Bottom Lane within Dire’s side. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Off_Lane]

| Faction | Off Lane path |
|---|---|
| Radiant | Starts at the north ramp and ends immediately before the north-east river ramp. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Off_Lane] |
| Dire | Starts at the south ramp and ends immediately before the south-east river ramp. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Off_Lane] |

| Off Lane trait |
|---|
| It is adjacent to the faction’s secondary jungle, which is unsuitable for early-game farming because it lacks creep camps and its few camps are large and ancient camps. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Off_Lane] |
| The Secret Shop is very close to the lane. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Off_Lane] |
| Its Tier 1 Tower is immediately before the river ramp, while the opposing team’s first tower is behind the 90° corner. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Off_Lane] |
| Without interference, lane creeps meet on the opposing team’s side of the lane, much closer to its tower. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Off_Lane] |
| The river ramps are directly in front of the Tier 1 Tower, making the lane highly vulnerable to ganks because enemies can use the ramp to cut off the path toward the tower. This positioning also provides easier access to the Rune in the river. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Off_Lane] |
| The Off Lane covers less than half of the Top or Bottom Lane—roughly 40%—because it ends well before the 90° turn. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Off_Lane] |
| It is generally considered more dangerous than the other Side Lane, hence “Hard Lane.” “Off Lane” describes a play style in which the hero is rarely seen last-hitting in the lane and instead remains “off the lane,” at the safest possible distance for gaining experience. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Off_Lane] |

## Laning

**Laning** is the act of staying in a lane. A laning hero gains gold and experience from dying creeps while protecting the nearest tower. Games generally begin with a laning phase in which all or most players start in a lane. Ganking, pushing, roaming, and other roles become relevant later, although players often return to laning for a steady income of experience and gold. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Laning]

## Laning Strategies

Because each lane has different advantages and disadvantages, teams can employ various laning strategies. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Laning_Strategies]

### Solo Lane

A **Solo Lane** contains only one player from the team. The hero does not share gold or experience with allies and can therefore farm much faster, but is exposed and highly susceptible to ganks. A solo laner may also struggle substantially against an opposing dual lane or trilane. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Solo_Lane]

Solo lanes are possible in every lane but are almost always used in the Mid Lane and are also common in the Off Lane. Solo Safe Lanes are rare. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Solo_Lane]

#### Solo Mid

The Mid Lane is the optimal solo lane for several reasons. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Solo_Mid]

| Reason |
|---|
| As the shortest lane, its creeps take less time to meet, making it the fastest lane for farming. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Solo_Mid] |
| It provides easy access to both Runes, the primary and secondary jungles, and both Side Lanes. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Solo_Mid] |
| The distance between each tower, including the teams’ mid Tier 1 Towers, is much shorter than in the Side Lanes. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Solo_Mid] |
| The river ramps provide safety and a high-ground advantage. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Solo_Mid] |
| The lane is relatively symmetric and therefore offers both teams an even playing field, unlike the other lanes. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Solo_Mid] |

The Middle Lane is usually reserved for key heroes and requires a high degree of skill. Its player contests last hits and denies to shift gold and experience in their favor, simultaneously limiting the enemy mid laner’s power and strengthening their own hero. A player who “wins” mid can gank the Side Lanes more successfully and push down the enemy Mid Lane tower, gaining map control for the entire team. Mid also revolves heavily around Rune control and avoiding enemy ganks. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Solo_Mid]

#### Solo Off

The Off Lane is also commonly used as a solo lane, but serves different purposes from solo mid. Because the Off Lane is dangerous, its solo player is expected to be underfarmed. Rather than pursuing last hits, the solo offlaner stays at a safe distance and absorbs experience from slain creeps, attempting to level as quickly as possible without dying. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Solo_Off]

Solo Off is usually assigned to heroes that prioritize experience and levels over gold and items, heroes that gain a large power spike from acquiring their ultimate, or heroes that have stats gain per level. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Solo_Off]

### Dual Lane

A **Dual Lane** contains two players from the team. Its usual setup pairs a farm-dependent hero focused on securing as many last hits as possible with a support who protects that hero, denies as many creeps as possible, and wards off enemies. They pursue kills only when an opportunity arises and the farming hero will not lose too many last hits. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Dual_Lane]

An aggressive dual lane instead has both players repeatedly attempt to kill the enemy laners, denying them as much laning time as possible. This style is usually combined with a quick push intended to destroy the enemy Tier 1 Tower as rapidly as possible. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Dual_Lane]

| Location | Typical use |
|---|---|
| Safe Lane | The carry-support dual lane is most common here because the lane provides the ideal environment for a carry to farm. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Dual_Lane] |
| Off Lane | Dual lanes usually use the aggressive setup, with both heroes trying to make the best of the lane. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Dual_Lane] |
| Mid Lane | Dual mid lanes are very rare. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Dual_Lane] |

### Tri-Lane

A **Tri-Lane** contains three players from the team. Like dual lanes, Tri-Lane strategies have two types. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Tri-Lane]

| Type | Composition and purpose |
|---|---|
| Defensive trilane | Three heroes occupy the Safe Lane. Two supports ensure that the third hero—the carry—receives all the farm without having to worry much about the enemy. They push enemy heroes back to deny them experience and gold while protecting the carry. Meanwhile, the supports can obtain some experience and gold by farming the jungle. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Tri-Lane] |
| Aggressive trilane | Three heroes occupy the Off Lane, pressuring the enemy Safe Lane and disrupting the jungle. Success largely depends on securing hero or tower kills. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/lanes@2329818#Tri-Lane] |
