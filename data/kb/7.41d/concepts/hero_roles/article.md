# Hero Roles

Heroes are suited to roles through the combination of their attributes, abilities, and items and how these shape the game. Although abilities can serve different purposes in different situations, roles define a hero’s expected playstyle and actions. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059]

## 1–5 Position System

The position system describes farm priority and, consequently, each hero’s expected contribution. Each team assigns one hero to every position from 1 to 5: position 1 has the highest farm priority and spends much of the match farming, while position 5 has the lowest and devotes time elsewhere. Positions are a major consideration during picking because heroes’ strengths and weaknesses must form a well-rounded composition. Most heroes can occupy multiple positions, and this flexibility is highly important in Captain’s Mode. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#1-5_Position_System]

### Position 1: Carry

The Carry is typically weakest early and most dependent on farm. They begin in the safe lane, where farming is easiest, accompanied by a support who protects them and enables their game. These heroes generally possess percentage-based abilities that make their autoattacks scale extremely well with items, produce high sustained DPS in the lategame, and can take Towers, Roshan, and the Tormentor. Midlaners depend on both items and levels. Their solo-experience advantage lets them quickly become the most powerful team member in the earlygame. They must hold their own in a 1v1 lane and pressure opponents early with strong ultimate abilities. ### Position 3: Offlaner

Offlaners occupy the opponent’s safelane, taking available farm while prioritizing pressure on the enemy Carry. They need enough resiliency to farm dangerous locations ignored by positions 1 and 2 and enough threat to turn a fight when help arrives. They are expected to form their team’s frontline while fights develop, so they typically purchase defensive items and teamfight auras. ### Position 4: Soft Support or Utility Support

Position 4 is typically the team’s most capable playmaker, continuously seeking high-impact kills and objectives early. After establishing lane control, this hero is often the first to move around the map, using a combination of control and damage. ### Position 5: Hard Support

The Hard Support is typically a powerful laner who uses abilities and high base stats to enable the Carry. These heroes generally have extremely strong ultimates but require little gold, allowing them to concentrate on creating safe farming opportunities or advantageous fights through strategic Observer Ward and Sentry Ward placement. They are frequent targets when out of position and therefore favor defensive items that help themselves and allies survive. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#1-5_Position_System]

## Official Roles

Official roles account for a hero’s physical stats, the contributions and nature of their abilities, where they should lane or jungle, and how much gold and how many items they need. The classifications are fairly rigid, but every hero can fill multiple roles through different choices, although some choices are wiser than others. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Official_Roles]

### Carry

> “Will become more useful later in the game if they gain a significant gold advantage.” [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Carry]

Carries can acquire the greatest offensive power as a game progresses. Their name reflects being “carried” by their team into the late game before assuming responsibility for ultimate victory. Substantial levels and items make them extremely powerful relative to other heroes. Once farmed, they excel at killing opponents, although carries such as Naga Siren may instead win through destroying enemy structures. They generally lack early power but must be powerful late, making items essential to their builds. Most rely on right-clicking with inherent attack power, usually augmented by abilities, and tend to have high base movement speed. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Carry]

Their abilities tend to be less useful at low levels, leaving them relatively weak early despite their late-game power. Many “hard” carries—those extremely dependent on items but also benefiting most from them—must farm almost incessantly prior to around 30-40 minutes. They should not participate in early skirmishes unless they can assist without danger; Spectre, for example, can contribute damage with Haunt without being in danger. Their team may consequently play much of the game without them, while a semi-carry, usually the solo mid, serves as the carry during the early to mid game, when a semi-carry has maximum relative power. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Carry]

Carries occur among all three primary attributes, although the hardest carries are almost entirely agility. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Carry]

| Agility | Inflict substantial damage in rapid succession and have very high limits for damage output. They tend to be ranged, although many are melee. They generally offer minimal utility beyond damage and are fragile, with exceptions to both tendencies. |

Carries are typically placed in a side lane with one or more supports to babysit them until they can farm independently. A select few, most notably Shadow Fiend, can occupy solo midlane for most of the game. Carries may instead take the solo safe lane—Radiant Bottom or Dire Top—when their team runs an aggressive tri-lane in the suicide lane—Radiant Top or Dire Bottom. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Carry]

As a rule, the more farm a carry needs to become effective, the more power they can attain relative to other carries. “Out-carrying” means having greater potential power to carry than another hero. Carries that require little farm can begin carrying early, but can and usually will be out-carried by a harder carry if the latter has time to amass the required gold and items. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Carry]

### Support

> “Can focus less on amassing gold and items, and more on using their abilities to gain an advantage for the team.” [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Support]

Supports keep allies alive and create opportunities for them to gain gold and experience. Their abilities commonly heal allies or disable enemies, emphasizing utility over damage, although supports can inflict huge damage in suitable situations. They are not highly item-dependent and generally buy only one or two items for personal use, spending the remainder of their gold on team-benefiting items. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Support]

| Team-benefiting purchase |
|---|
| Animal Courier |
| Observer Ward |
| Sentry Ward |
| Smoke of Deceit |

[corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Support]

Supports are normally paired with the Carry at the start because the Carry is the team’s weakest and most gold-hungry early member, while supports are strongest early. A support should forfeit kills to teammates who depend more heavily on items and perform the kill only when no ally can do so. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Support]

### Nuker

> “Can quickly kill enemy heroes using high damage spells with low cooldowns.” [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Nuker]

Nukers produce fast, strong, and/or sustainable spell or magical damage through single-target or area-of-effect spells. Because area damage comes mainly from nukes, AoE Nukers can create a major damage-per-second advantage in team fights by harming multiple heroes simultaneously. They also push effectively by rapidly dispatching enemy creeps. Burst Nukers such as Lina can kill or grievously wound an enemy immediately with a single fast spell combination before they can react, making a team fight one-sided from the outset. Mana costs and cooldowns dictate usage: low-cost and/or low-cooldown nukes can be spammed in lanes or ganks, while higher-cost and higher-cooldown nukes are generally reserved for large confrontations. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Nuker]

### Disabler

> “Has a guaranteed disable for one or more of their spells.” [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Disabler]

Disablers emphasize reliable crowd control against either single targets or areas. Supportive heroes, whose abilities are not improved by items, often lane with carries to frustrate attempts on their lives. Carries generally have weak disabling power, although Faceless Void is an exception. Heroes with powerful disables tend to have low base movement speed, giving enemies time to react, but mobility items can bypass this limitation. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Disabler]

### Durable

> “Has the ability to last longer in team fights.” [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Durable]

Durable heroes, or “tanks,” can potentially sustain large amounts of incoming enemy damage. Their durability commonly comes from health, health regeneration, armor, or magic resistance, supplemented by abilities that mitigate damage, avoid it completely, or disable assailants. Strength heroes almost invariably fit this standard because strength grants health, although heroes of any attribute can qualify. Durable heroes usually possess one or more abilities improved by their being difficult to kill. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Durable]

Most Durable heroes cannot literally force enemies to attack them; Axe and Legion Commander are exceptions. They can nevertheless absorb most enemy damage in a team fight through the following tactics. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Durable]

| Tactic | Details |
|---|---|
| Produce a severe AoE effect | The effect can make killing the Durable practically necessary. Undying’s Flesh Golem, for example, deals damage over time to hostile units in its radius. |
| Engage first | Enemies may unload abilities and items because they cannot see the Durable’s allies and assume an easy solo kill, cannot reach the allies because they are too far away, or inexperiencedly attack the first hostile target in sight. The Durable will probably survive because of high durability; when allies arrive, many enemy abilities are on cooldown, making the battle more favorable. This does not always work against seasoned opponents. |
| Force enemy attacks | An ability can force enemies to attack the Durable instead of allies. Axe and Legion Commander are the only current in-game examples, while Winter Wyvern has a similar ability. |
| Be the only readily attackable target | This works best when the Durable’s allies are ranged heroes. |

[corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Durable]

In summary, the role combines high survivability with frequently initiating team battles and, ideally, manipulating the enemy’s damage output. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Durable]

### Escape

> “Has the ability to quickly avoid death.” [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Escape]

Escape heroes have one or more mechanisms that let them, and sometimes their allies, avoid damage and abilities while retreating or repositioning during a team fight or gank. They are especially suited to soloing the “suicide lane” or short lane because they can turn otherwise inevitable death into only a temporary delay in farm. ### Initiator

> “Good at starting a team fight.” [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Initiator]

Initiators can safely and advantageously begin team fights. They typically possess strong area-of-effect damage, an area disable, or an ability that affects enemy positioning. Many depend completely on a positional item to initiate properly, while some do not. Initiators are commonly Durable, but this is not required; fragile heroes can also initiate effectively. The role becomes crucial late because an effective initiation can win a team fight and a poor one can lose it. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Initiator]

## Unofficial Roles

### Hard Carry

A hard carry is especially dependent on acquiring items, unlike softer carries that gain substantial damage from abilities and can be dangerous without gold. Hard-carry abilities scale closely with items and are weak without farm. Because hard carries cannot have farm early, they are generally laned with disabling supports whose strong early abilities counterbalance the hard carry’s extreme weakness. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Hard_carry]

As hard carries gain gold and levels, they progressively become more dangerous and reach maximum relative effectiveness later than other heroes, giving them a huge advantage if the game lasts long enough. Item dependence directly correlates with effectiveness after obtaining those items: Anti-Mage with three carry items is stronger than Medusa with the same amount, but with six items Medusa uses them much better, making her the harder carry. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Hard_carry]

### Semi-Carry

Semi-carries, like hard carries, specialize in killing enemy heroes. Unlike hard carries, they need relatively little farm and depend primarily on levels, so they usually take the middle lane to gain levels rapidly. They are most dominant in the midgame, before either team’s true carry gains enough farm to supersede them. A successful semi-carry establishes map control and impedes the opposing Carry’s farm, allowing their own Carry to amass items safely. Although long recognized by the community, “semi-carry” is not a Valve-recognized role, likely because its definition is highly ambiguous. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Semi-carry]

A successful semi-carry may have respectable attack damage depending on the hero, and every semi-carry scales at least moderately with items. By the end of the middle game, however, they typically transition toward utility—not support—because hard carries begin to outstrip their damage and capabilities. To compensate for their damage “falling off,” they often build utility items. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Semi-carry]

### Ganker

Gankers have abilities that deliver long-duration crowd control and/or immense early- and midgame damage. They tend to be mobile and combine physical damage, magical damage, and disables. Their goal during the laning phase is to create an early advantage by killing enemy heroes in their proper lanes, with restricting the opposing Carry’s farm and levels as their most important task. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Ganker]

The role often overlaps with semi-carry because a successful Ganker can become a semi-carry through a large gold and level advantage. Their abilities usually scale less effectively, causing them to suffer more in the late game and creating a need for a hard carry. Nevertheless, an exceptionally successful Ganker may remain a greater threat at the game’s conclusion than an allied hard carry. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Ganker]

### Offlaner

Offlaners possess abilities allowing them to solo the “suicide lane”: top for Radiant and bottom for Dire. Including an Offlaner permits a Jungler or a tri-lane with less risk of losing that lane. Offlaners may sacrifice substantial farm merely to survive and can remain on the back foot for much of the laning stage. Escape is particularly valuable because their first priority is not dying in lane. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Offlaner]

Against skilled opponents, Offlaners may become extremely under-leveled relative to other heroes. A hard carry is therefore generally an unwise choice because they may not receive enough farm to acquire the items needed to snowball into the late-game. ## Jobs in the Game

Roles can also be considered through heroes’ effects on the course of a game. These jobs are separate from starting lanes and farm allocation. A good composition can perform every job or, at minimum, counter enemies performing them. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Jobs_in_the_game]

### Laning

#### Lane Pushing

Lane pushers rapidly destroy creep waves and move quickly between lanes—or attack a lane from across the map—to pressure enemy towers. This forces responses, can create ganking opportunities, and at minimum makes enemies disengage their own attack to answer the waves. Lane pushing is countered by lane de-pushing or ganking because strong pushers are often alone. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Lane_pushing]

#### Lane De-pushing

Lane de-pushers safely eliminate creep waves defensively even while the enemy team is present. Sniper’s Shrapnel, for example, can destroy creeps without putting Sniper in danger. Strong initiators such as Gankers counter de-pushers, as do tower hitters capable of damaging objectives without creep-wave assistance. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Lane_de-pushing]

### Fighting

#### Ganking

Ganking punishes out-of-position enemies through quick pickoffs. Ganking heroes commonly possess abilities that let them safely move around the enemy team to reach a target, together with a nuke or burst damage that finishes the target quickly. Strong team fighting counters Gankers by preventing them from reaching the heroes most vulnerable to ganks. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Ganking]

#### Team Fighting

Team-fighting heroes can substantially affect fights involving multiple heroes, including through large area-of-effect disables. They can often sustain or avoid massive amounts of damage while damaging enemy carries. Strong lane pushing counters team-fighting compositions by forcing them to split up or retreat to defend. ### Objectives

#### Tower Hitters

Heroes that safely damage towers over time, or possess burst physical or pure damage abilities, are effective tower takers. They are commonly countered by direct fighting—either ganking or team fighting that prevents objective damage—or by enough lane push to remove nearby creep waves and activate backdoor protection. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Tower_hitters]

#### Roshan Killers

## Trivia

### Jungler

> “Can farm effectively from neutral creeps inside the jungle early in the game.” [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Jungler]

Junglers efficiently farm neutral creeps from the start instead of laning. This creates two solo lanes, allowing two allies rather than one to receive solo farm. The ability to jungle occurs among heroes of every attribute class and role. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Jungler]

| Typical jungling ability |
|---|
| Convert neutral creeps |
| Summon minions |
| Sustain the hero through moderate damage from jungle creeps |

[corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Jungler]

Although two solo lanes plus a Jungler provide a significant gold and experience advantage, they increase exposure to enemy ganks and can weaken the side lanes. The role was removed in January 10, 2023 Patch. [corpus:liquipedia_dota2/hero_roles@2374059#Jungler]
